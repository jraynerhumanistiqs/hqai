## PF21
<!-- Record-keeping obligations -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Pick one system for pay, hours, leave and super records, and keep everything for seven years, including records for people who have left. The format matters less than being complete and consistent. A mix of spreadsheets, texted rosters and paper timesheets often misses required content. Records must be in English, legible, accurate and easy to produce on request. When you fix an error, note the correction clearly rather than overwriting it. Common gaps are leave balances, super contribution details, and the history of each person's classification and pay rate. The trap is thinking poor records only risk a fine. If someone claims they were underpaid and your records are inadequate, the burden shifts to you to prove you paid correctly. Missing records can lose a wage dispute on their own. If you find gaps, especially for ex-staff, get advice before trying to fill them in after the fact.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - Record-keeping and pay slips fact sheet
Fair Work Act 2009 ss535-536
Fair Work Regulations 2009 regs 3.31-3.46

### escalation_guidance
Get advice if you find missing or incomplete records, receive a Fair Work Ombudsman request, or face any underpayment claim. Poor records shift the burden of proof onto you, and reconstructing records after the fact can make things worse.

## PF22
<!-- Pay slip compliance -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: FALSE
- status: review

### answer_text
Pull up your last pay slip and check it against what the rules require. It must go out within one working day of pay day and show your business name and ABN, the employee's name, the pay period, the payment date, and the gross and net amounts. Any bonus, loading, allowance, penalty rate or overtime must appear as its own line. Where you made a super contribution, show the fund and account details. The trap is the lump sum. Showing one gross pay figure is a breach even if every dollar paid is correct. Basic payroll software usually gets the format right, but manual payroll or software not built for Australian awards often does not. Civil penalties apply per breach, so one gap repeated every pay run for every employee adds up fast.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - Record-keeping and pay slips fact sheet
Fair Work Ombudsman - Pay slips and record-keeping
Fair Work Act 2009 s536
Fair Work Regulations 2009 reg 3.46

### escalation_guidance
If checking your pay slips shows that allowances, penalty rates or overtime were not actually paid, treat it as a suspected underpayment and get advice before you fix it. The same applies if the Fair Work Ombudsman has contacted you about your records.

## PF24
<!-- Super guarantee - rate, OTE, timing -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Work out super on each person's ordinary time earnings (OTE), not their gross pay, and pay it to the right fund on time every pay cycle. Casuals get super too, the same as any other eligible employee. The super guarantee rate has reached its final legislated level, with no further increases scheduled, so check the current figure with the ATO rather than relying on memory. OTE is the trap. Overtime is usually left out, but many allowances count, and bonuses and leave loading each have their own rules. Applying super to gross pay can leave you over or under, depending on the pay mix. Before you put a new starter into your default fund, do the ATO stapled fund check. A shortfall is not fixed by quietly paying late. It triggers the Superannuation Guarantee Charge, which costs much more than the missed amount.

### source_refs
Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (Cth)

### escalation_guidance
If you find super has been calculated on the wrong base or paid late or short, get advice from your accountant or the ATO before you correct it. A shortfall is an underpayment and brings Superannuation Guarantee Charge exposure.

## PF25
<!-- Payday Super (biggest 2026 payroll change) -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Check now whether your payroll software or provider supports Payday Super, and plan your cash flow as if super leaves your account with every pay run. Under Payday Super, you pay super at the same time as wages, within a short window after each payday, instead of quarterly. The quarterly gap many small businesses quietly relied on for cash flow goes away. The trap is treating this as a timing tweak. The calculation base also changes to 'Qualifying Earnings', which is not exactly the same as the ordinary time earnings base you use now, and the maximum contribution base moves from quarterly to annual. Late or short payments trigger a faster superannuation guarantee charge. The charge itself becomes tax-deductible, but penalties and interest do not. Confirm the start date and exact payment window with the ATO.

### source_refs
ATO - Super guarantee penalties (Super for employers > Quarterly super to 30 June 2026 > Missed and late super guarantee payments)

### escalation_guidance
If you have paid super late or short, or are unsure what counts as Qualifying Earnings for your staff, get advice from the ATO or a registered tax or BAS agent before the next pay run. Unpaid super is an underpayment, and under the new regime the charge triggers faster.

## PM17
<!-- Retention & turnover -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: FALSE
- status: review

### answer_text
Start by finding out why people leave and why they stay, before you assume it is pay. Workload is the reason employers most often give for people leaving, and in a small team one manager's habits can drive most of your turnover. You do not need survey software. Hold a short stay conversation with each person every few months, ask the same few questions, and have a proper exit conversation when someone resigns, so "it's not working out" turns into something you can act on. Keep a simple record of who left, when and why. Then spend where it counts: learning and development, flexible work and wellbeing support are the levers employers use most. The trap is reading lower turnover as good news. Engagement is weak, and people may be staying because they are worried, not because they are happy.

### source_refs
AHRI - Quarterly Australian Work Outlook (Q1 2025 report)
AHRI - Quarterly Australian Work Outlook (Q3 2025 report)

### escalation_guidance
If a stay or exit conversation raises bullying, harassment, discrimination, unpaid hours or underpayment, or a workload complaint affecting someone's health, stop treating it as a retention issue and get advice before you respond. Those are complaints with legal exposure, and mishandling them can turn a resignation into a claim.
