## PF5
<!-- Contractor high-income threshold & opt-out notice -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Do not draft or issue an opt-out notice yourself. It must come from the contractor, in writing, and it is only available if their earnings are above the contractor high income threshold. Check the current threshold on the Fair Work Commission site rather than relying on last year's number, because it is indexed every year. The trap is the myth that high pay alone makes someone a contractor. It does not. Without a valid notice in place, the whole-of-relationship test still decides whether they are really an employee, however well they are paid. If a contractor gives you a notice, file a copy with the date you received it and a record showing their earnings were over the threshold at that time. They can revoke it once. If they do, treat the relationship as exposed to the full test again and review how the work actually runs.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - Opting out of the whole of relationship test
Fair Work Commission - Contractor high income threshold
Fair Work Act 2009 s15AB

### escalation_guidance
Any engagement that looks employee-like in practice, a contractor who has revoked their notice, or any doubt about whether earnings clear the current threshold needs advice before you rely on contractor status. Getting classification wrong can create underpayment and sham contracting exposure.

## PF18
<!-- Compassionate/bereavement leave (incl. miscarriage) -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
When someone tells you a family member has died or they have had a miscarriage, approve the leave. Under the NES, every employee, including casuals, gets 2 days' compassionate leave each time a qualifying event happens. That covers the death or life-threatening illness or injury of an immediate family or household member, a stillbirth, or a miscarriage. Miscarriage is covered for either partner, however the pregnancy was achieved. Permanent staff are paid. Casuals take it unpaid. The trap is treating the 2 days as a yearly bank. It applies per occasion, so it can recur within weeks. The other traps are assuming casuals get nothing or that miscarriage is not covered. Both are wrong, and refusing is an NES breach. Who counts as family can be broader than you expect, so check before saying no. 'Bereavement leave' is the everyday name for the same entitlement.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - Compassionate and bereavement leave
Fair Work Ombudsman - Sick and carer's leave and compassionate leave fact sheet
Fair Work Act 2009 ss104-106

### escalation_guidance
Anything involving a miscarriage or stillbirth, a refusal of leave, a dispute about who counts as immediate family or household, or any performance or discipline step close in time to the leave - get advice before you act. A wrong call is an NES contravention, and action near a pregnancy loss risks a general protections or discrimination claim.

## PF20
<!-- Community service leave (jury/emergency) -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Treat jury duty and emergency volunteering as two different things, because the pay rules differ. If a full-time or part-time employee is called for jury duty, the NES requires you to pay make-up pay for the first 10 days. That is the difference between their base rate and whatever the court pays them. Casuals can take the leave but do not get make-up pay. Emergency volunteering, like a volunteer firefighter or SES call-out, is unpaid leave any employee can take, casuals included, and there is no cap on how long it lasts. The trap is assuming all leave like this is unpaid and skipping the jury make-up pay, or refusing a call-out from a recognised emergency body. Either is an NES breach. If you have volunteers on staff, plan cover before bushfire and flood season.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - Community service leave
Fair Work Ombudsman - Community service leave fact sheet
Fair Work Act 2009 ss108-112

### escalation_guidance
Get advice if you have withheld jury duty make-up pay, refused or penalised someone for emergency service leave, or are unsure whether a volunteer group counts as a recognised emergency management body. Missed make-up pay is an underpayment, and refusing leave is an NES contravention.

## PM19
<!-- Ad hoc training & key-person/succession risk -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: FALSE
- status: review

### answer_text
Start this week by writing down who knows something nobody else does. If one person leaving tomorrow would leave you stuck, that is your key-person risk. Then pick cheap ways to spread that knowledge. Pair someone with them for job shadowing, give out stretch tasks, set up informal mentoring, and use low-cost short courses where they fill a real gap. Get critical processes written down, even roughly. A shared document beats everything living in one head. Name a likely internal successor for each key role now, not when someone resigns or you plan to exit the business. The trap is treating training as a cost and succession as a big-company thing. This is mainly a business continuity risk, not a legal one. But regulated industries can have licensing or CPD requirements, and traineeships can carry their own compliance layer under the relevant award.

### source_refs
AHRI - Quarterly Australian Work Outlook, Q3 2025 (https://www.ahri.com.au/wp-content/uploads/AHRI-WorkOutlook-Report-2025-Q3.pdf)

### escalation_guidance
Get advice if the role involves mandatory licensing or CPD, or you are taking on a trainee or apprentice, because missing those obligations creates compliance exposure. Also get advice if training or succession changes affect someone's pay, duties or job security.
