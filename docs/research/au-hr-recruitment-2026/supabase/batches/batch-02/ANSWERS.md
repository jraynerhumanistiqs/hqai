# Answers - batch batch-02


## PF13
<!-- The NES as the compliance floor -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Treat the National Employment Standards as the minimum every employee gets, whatever your award, agreement or contract says. The list covers maximum weekly hours, flexible work requests, parental leave, various paid and unpaid leave, public holidays, notice and redundancy pay, casual conversion rights, and the Fair Work Information Statement. Check your contracts and award against it. The trap is assuming that if your award says nothing about something, there is no entitlement. Parental leave, notice and redundancy exist in the NES with no award equivalent, so your award may never prompt you. A contract cannot sign the NES away, because any term that gives less has no effect. Casuals do not get every NES right in the same way, so check which apply before you assume. Give every employee the Fair Work Information Statement, because failing to can attract civil penalties.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - National Employment Standards
Fair Work Ombudsman - Introduction to the National Employment Standards fact sheet
Fair Work Commission - National Employment Standards
Fair Work Act 2009 Part 2-2

### escalation_guidance
If you find a contract or award term that gives less than the NES, or a staff member has missed out on an NES entitlement such as notice, redundancy pay or parental leave, get advice before fixing it, as this can be an underpayment. Anything involving parental leave or redundancy also needs a human.

## PF14
<!-- Annual leave - accrual, excess, cashing out -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Before you direct anyone to take leave or pay it out, check your award, because it sets the rules for both. Annual leave does not expire. Full-time staff build up four weeks a year (five for some shiftworkers). It accrues progressively, and unused leave carries over indefinitely. Part-timers accrue on a pro-rata basis. If someone is sitting on a large balance, your award may let you direct them to take excess leave, but you have to follow the award's process and notice rules. Cashing out is only allowed where the award permits it, and only with a written agreement and proper records. The trap is the quick informal payout. A cash-out without the right paperwork can be treated as never having happened, so you may still owe the leave you thought you paid. Also check whether leave loading applies under the award or contract.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - National Employment Standards
Fair Work Ombudsman - Paid annual leave & sick & carer's leave - Frequently Asked Questions
Fair Work Act 2009 ss87-94

### escalation_guidance
Get advice before you act if a leave payout has already been made without a written agreement, or if you are unsure whether your award allows you to direct excess leave or cash it out. A wrong move here can leave you still owing leave you thought you had paid, plus a record-keeping breach.

## PF15
<!-- Personal/carer's leave & the casual gap -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Treat sick leave and carer's leave as one balance. Full-time and part-time staff build up paid personal/carer's leave as they work: 10 days for each year of service, pro-rata for part-timers. Whether they are ill or injured, or caring for a family or household member, it comes out of the same bank. Unused leave carries over year to year. Unlike annual leave, it generally is not paid out when they leave. Casuals do not get the paid version, but they can take 2 days' unpaid carer's leave per occasion. You can ask for evidence such as a medical certificate or statutory declaration, but the request has to be reasonable. The trap is demanding a certificate for every single-day absence without checking your award and policy. Refusing valid leave or asking for unreasonable evidence can breach the NES.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - Sick and carer's leave
Fair Work Ombudsman - Paid sick and carer's leave
Fair Work Commission - NES Personal and Carer's Leave fact sheet
Fair Work Act 2009 ss96-107

### escalation_guidance
Get advice before you refuse leave, question a pattern of absences, or discipline anyone over sick or carer's leave. Getting it wrong can breach the NES or look like adverse action against someone who is ill or caring for family.

## PF10
<!-- Correct base pay rates & keeping up with wage rises -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Before your first pay run after 1 July, check every employee's rate against the new minimum for their award and classification. The Fair Work Commission's Annual Wage Review lifts award minimums from 1 July, and awards can also change mid-year. Use the Fair Work Pay Calculator or your award, not last year's rate card. The trap is assuming someone paid above award last year is still above award now. Recent increases have been large, so that margin may have shrunk or gone. Also check casual loading, increments, and any piece rates or allowances that move with the base rate. Timing matters: the new rate applies from the effective date, so even a short lag is an underpayment. If you find one, fix it and back-pay to that date.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - About awards
Fair Work Ombudsman - Pay Calculator

### escalation_guidance
If you find you have paid below the current award rate, or you plan to absorb a wage rise into an existing above-award payment, get advice before you act. Getting either wrong creates underpayment exposure that runs from the effective date.

## PF11
<!-- Penalty rates, overtime & allowances -->

- jurisdiction: federal
- is_escalate_default: TRUE
- status: review

### answer_text
Pull up the award that covers each person's actual work and read its penalty, overtime and allowance clauses before you finalise the roster, not after payday. Most awards add extra payments on top of the base rate for weekend, public holiday, evening and night work, for overtime, and for allowances such as uniform, tool, travel, meal or first aid. Each one has its own trigger. Overtime is the trap. Depending on the award it can start under a daily, weekly or rostered-hours test, so a simple weekly hours cut-off is not a safe rule. Some allowances are owed regardless of hours worked, or because of a specific duty like handling cash. Check amounts with the Fair Work Pay Calculator rather than from memory. Missing these is the biggest single driver of underpayments, and it counts even when it was an honest mistake.

### source_refs
Fair Work Ombudsman - Pay Calculator
Fair Work Act 2009 s45
JB Solicitors - Fair Work Awards (https://jbsolicitors.com.au/employment-awards/)

### escalation_guidance
If you find you have missed penalty rates, overtime or allowances for past shifts, get advice before you fix it. Back-pay calculation and how you handle it both carry underpayment exposure. Also get advice if an enterprise agreement may apply or you are unsure which award covers the work.
