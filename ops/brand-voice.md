# HQ.ai Brand Voice

The single calibration sample, lifted from the live product (Strategy Coach blurb):

> "Your AI-guided sounding board for business strategy. Talk through hiring plans, revenue targets, and operating decisions with a coach that knows your business profile, your awards exposure, and your team. We're putting the finishing touches on it - it'll land in the next release."

That's the bar. Every piece of customer-facing copy should feel like it could sit next to that line without clashing.

## What that line is doing right

- Talks to the reader, not about the product. "Your", "you", "talk through".
- Specific nouns owners actually use: hiring plans, revenue targets, awards exposure.
- One-line promise, then a soft status note. No hype.
- Uses a plain hyphen for the aside, not an em-dash.
- No buzzwords. No "leverage", "empower", "robust", "seamless", "synergy".
- Warm, not chummy. Confident, not boastful.

## Voice rules

1. **First person from the AI.** "I'll walk you through it." Not "The system will provide guidance." (The old example here was "I'll draft the letter", which also promised document generation - withdrawn Aug 2026, chat is Q&A only. Do not promise it.)
2. **Plain English at year-9 reading level.** If a sentence needs a comma to survive, cut it in half.
3. **Specific over generic.** "Construction sub-contractor in Penrith" beats "small business owner".
4. **Show the work, not the workflow.** Owners care about the answer, not the architecture.
5. **Australian English.** Organise, not organize. Programme stays as program for software. Use "around" not "circa".
6. **Anti-corporate.** Read it aloud. If you'd cringe saying it to a publican, rewrite it.
7. **No em-dashes or en-dashes.** Plain hyphens only in all customer-facing copy.
8. **No emojis** unless the channel demands it (e.g. specific LinkedIn formats) and the human approves.
9. **The AI never practises law.** It does not cite sections, decide classifications, calculate what someone owes, or advise on a live matter. It is grounded in Australian workplace law; it does not rule on it. Full rule in `## The legal line` below, and it outranks every other rule in this file including the voice ones.

## Words to avoid

leverage, empower, unlock, supercharge, revolutionary, game-changing, world-class, best-in-class, cutting-edge, robust, seamless, synergy, ecosystem, holistic, deep dive, circle back, low-hanging fruit, move the needle, paradigm shift, pivot, bandwidth, learnings, ideate, action (as a verb).

## Words and phrases that fit

"I'll walk you through it." "Three minutes, no signup." "Ask in plain
English, get a plain-English answer in thirty seconds." "Tell me your
industry once - you're not briefing a new consultant every quarter." "If
you've been hit with a Fair Work claim, start here." "When it's the hard
twenty per cent, your advisor picks it up."

## Phrases that do NOT fit (and why)

These were in this file until Nov 2026 and they are the exact failure to
watch for: every one has the AI performing a legal act. See `## The legal
line`.

- "I checked the award. You're under by $3.42 an hour." - the AI running an
  award assessment and stating a shortfall.
- "Your bartender's classified wrong - here's the fix and the back-pay
  number." - a classification determination plus a back-pay calculation.
- "The Fair Work claim arrives Monday - here's what to send before then." -
  advice on a live matter.

They read well, which is why they lasted. Good copy that makes the wrong
claim is more dangerous than bad copy, not less.

## Five-second test

Show the headline to someone who's never heard of HQ.ai. They have five seconds. Can they tell you:
1. What the thing does
2. Who it's for
3. Why it matters today

If not, rewrite.

## CTA test

Read the CTA aloud. Does it sound like something you'd actually click? "Ask my first question" beats "Schedule a consultation". "Try it on a real question" beats "Try it free".

A CTA is copy too, so `## The legal line` applies to it. "Show me what I owe" and "Get my free award check" were the examples here until Nov 2026; both promise a determination the AI does not make. Promise the action the reader takes, not a legal verdict they will receive.

## The legal line (read this before you write a word about the law)

HQ.ai is not a law firm and the AI is not an adviser. Copy never shows it
citing a section, making a determination, or telling someone what the law
requires of them. The product itself is careful about this - every answer
in the app carries "General guidance only - not legal advice" and routes
the hard questions to a human Humanistiqs advisor. Marketing that oversells
what the AI does writes a cheque the product deliberately refuses to cash.

What you MAY say:

- Scope, as a statement about grounding: "grounded in Australian workplace
  law", "built on the Fair Work Act, the NES and the modern awards". This
  describes what the AI reads, not what it rules on.
- Anything about busywork, time, cost and not repeating yourself. This is
  the actual pitch and it needs no legal framing at all.
- That a human advisor handles the hard 20 per cent. That is the product,
  and it is also the honest answer to "but is it giving me legal advice?".

What you may NOT say:

- The AI citing law: "I checked s.117 of the Fair Work Act".
- The AI making a determination: "your bartender is classified wrong",
  "you're underpaying by $3.42 an hour", "you owe $4,100 in back pay".
- The AI advising on a live matter: "here's what to send before Monday".
- Anything that reads as legal advice, compliance certification, or a
  guarantee of a legal outcome. No "stay compliant, guaranteed", no "avoid
  Fair Work claims", no "we'll make sure you're legal".

Editorial content (a blog post, an explainer) can state a fact about the
law, because that is journalism, not advice. When it does: attribute it to
the source, not to HQ.ai. "The NES gives 10 days paid family and domestic
violence leave (Fair Work Ombudsman, [date])" is fine. "We'll tell you your
leave entitlements" is not. If you cannot source a legal statement, cut it
- do not soften it and ship it.

The test: could a reader come away believing HQ.ai told them what the law
required and they relied on it? If yes, rewrite.
